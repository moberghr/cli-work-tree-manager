# Time (each day's hours per ticket)

The **Time** tab (`#/time`, `g h`, `TimeTab`): each workday's hours per Jira ticket, as work suggests them and as you change them. `core/time/`: `allocate.ts` (pure: `suggestDay` — the timesheet tool's rules as `TimeSettings`, config `time`: `dayHours` 7.5, Claude minutes × `multiplier` 5 capped at `capHours` 7, `stepHours` 0.25, at least `minHours` 0.5 a ticket, a ticket touched with no measured time (a commit, an issue moved) gets the minimum, the rest to `gapTicket`, a day off to `timeOffTicket`; never over the day — too many tickets trim the biggest first, then drop the smallest; `issueKeys`, `isWorkday` with `holidays`), `time-view.ts` (pure, shared with the demo: the day as shown — `dayWireOf`, `daysWireOf`, `activityOf`, `sessionTicket`: a session's Jira key, else the key in its branch or name; the suggestion is worked out at read time from the stored evidence and the settings now, so a changed setting applies at once; `parseEntries`), `time-days.ts` (`buildDay`: a day's evidence — each session's Claude minutes that day from `sessionWorkTime`, your commits in every enrolled repo by its `user.email` (`--fixed-strings --author=<email>`: an email isn't a pattern, and whole — `an@corp` isn't `ivan@corp`; `--exclude=refs/stash`, and a stash's own commits by subject, left out; one `git log` per repo serves a run's days, `commitLog`; a repo whose git fails fails the read, so the day keeps its commits, a folder that's gone is skipped; the reading is injectable, `TimeIo`), on the day they were written (author date: a rebase or amend counts on the day of the work; GitHub's squash merges left out — `commitsWrittenOn`), keys only of your projects (`time.projects`, else your sessions' and moved issues'), issues you moved (`status CHANGED BY currentUser() DURING …` through acli), titles — with the I/O passed in, `time-deps.ts` the real one; a source that fails this time (acli, Outlook, git) keeps what the day had from it, so a passing failure never moves the hours), `time-store.ts` (state.db `time_days`, schema v9: evidence and titles, and apart from them your rows (`edited`), `dayOff` and what was posted to Tempo (`posted`, with work's worklog ids), which a rebuild keeps; a stored `posted` that isn't whole makes the row unreadable rather than a crash; `work state --export` writes them in `newer-state.json`), `time-keeper.ts` (work web's job, Activity kind `time`: today after a turn ends (two minutes later) and every 30 min, every day from `listFrom` (the 1st of the month, or `catchUpDays` (14) back when that's earlier — so the whole month is there to post at its end; the work-time reader reaches as far, `reachFor`) last built before it ended — never built (weekends and holidays too: worked with work web closed), only edited, or built while it was still going, so late turns count after a night with work web closed (`needsBuild`, `dayEnd`); one build of a day at a time (a Gather while the keeper builds builds again after it — `buildDay(…, {again})` — rather than answering with what was read before the click); the AI step's candidate titles aren't kept with the day, only those of tickets it placed something on; it's shown at most `CANDIDATES_SHOWN` (50) beyond the day's own — the hints first —, and asked again only when the day's items or own tickets change, not your backlog; Jira's day searches return up to `JIRA_DAY_LIMIT` (500); asked while it runs (a turn ended, Outlook connected), it runs once more after, since that run read before; each run starts fresh (`TimeDeps.fresh`; after a turn only what a turn changes — Claude minutes, commits — the half-hourly run and a Gather everything: Teams, your assigned issues, a day's meetings, its Jira and its tickets' titles are kept until then) and a read then serves the run's days (a session's working time, a repo's commits, the chats, your assigned issues; 30 min at most), a few sessions at a time, and only sessions that could have worked that day (made by its end, not archived before it); it never rejects (work web fires it and forgets), a failure is said in Activity; a session the day had keeps its minutes when they can't be read now (deleted, a failed read, or a day past Claude Code's ~30 days of transcripts, `TRANSCRIPTS_KEPT_DAYS`) — a successful read of none is believed, so a figure measured too high comes down; a day that fails doesn't stop the rest, and the days built are still announced. Days are counted on the calendar (`addDays`), never in 24 h steps, which go wrong on a DST day). Routes (`time-routes.ts`): `GET /api/time?from&to`, `GET /api/time/:day`, `PUT /api/time/:day` (`{entries}` — null = back to the suggestion — or `{dayOff}`), `POST /api/time/:day/rebuild` (a build that fails says why); only real days, at most 92 at once (`MAX_RANGE_DAYS`: a range from year 1 held up every request), the list by default as far back as the keeper builds — `timeRange` and `dayChange` (`time-view.ts`) read requests for both servers; each broadcasts `time-changed`. The demo serves a month of made-up days (`demo-time.ts`), a pretend Outlook sign-in and posting.

**The Month view** (‹ month › and **Days | Month** on the tab; Days lists the month on screen): `time-month.ts` (pure, shared with the demo and the SPA: `monthWireOf` → `TimeMonthWire` — tickets × the month's days up to today, each ticket's total, and the **post plan**: the days that go, the rest with why (`leftOutWhy`: today, a placeholder ticket, already in Tempo, nothing to post, not gathered, a weekend or holiday you didn't save rows for — its suggestion fills a whole day with the gap ticket), the hours per ticket across what goes, the short days; a weekend shows only with something on it, `somethingOn`, the day list's rule), `GET /api/time/month/:month` (`monthWire`), `POST /api/time/month/:month/gather` (202; `month-gather.ts`: a past month's days the keeper didn't reach, one after another, `time-changed` after each, one gather of a month at a time, a failure kept for the view). **Post N days to Tempo…** (`TimeMonth.tsx`): a confirm with the plan's totals, then each day through its own `POST /api/time/:day/post` (Tempo's day read first, as for one day), one after another from the page, with progress, Stop after this day, and one line on how it went (`monthPostSummary`, `state/time-month.ts`). A cell can be typed in (`cellEdit`: that ticket's hours on that day, empty or 0 takes it out; the day's rows saved as yours, `PUT /api/time/:day`). Days or Month is remembered per browser (`readTimeView`). While the Month view is on screen the dashboard tells the Ctrl+K assistant the month (`AssistantView.month` → `assistantContextMonth`), and its prompt hook describes it as it is at each prompt (`describeMonthNow` → `describeTimeMonth`, fenced like a day); **Ask about this month** opens it. It can change days (`work timesheet set/reset/off`) and gather them again (`gather <day|YYYY-MM>`), asking first — none of those are pre-allowed.

**How each row got its hours** (`DaySuggestion.why`, `suggestDay`): what it measured (Claude minutes × `multiplier`, meetings 1:1, only touched: `minHours`), × a story-points factor for a task, a cut when the tickets come to more than the room (the cap, or the day less what the gap ticket keeps), raised to a minimum, trimmed so the day adds up, and for the gap ticket the rest of the day and any rows dropped for room. `dayWireOf` adds what it came from (`sourcesOf`: sessions, commits, meetings, chats, and whether the AI step placed them) → `TimeDayWire.why` / `TimeMonthDay.why`: under each row in Days, on a Month cell's hover, in the assistant's description; your own rows say "Your hours" with what work suggested. **Tasks vs service desk** (config, nothing project-specific in code): `time.deskProjects` (e.g. `["SSD"]`) keep the rules above; any other project's ticket but the gap and time-off tickets is a task (`isTask`): at least `time.taskMinHours` a day (null: off), and its story points weigh its share — × (1 + `time.pointsWeight` (0.25) × (points − 1)) — read from Jira's `time.storyPointsField` (`customfield_…`, each site its own; `issueNumberField`, one `acli … view` per task — search can't return custom fields — `pointsOf`), stored with the day (`TimeDayRecord.points`, kept when Jira can't answer).

**Posting** (`tempo.ts`, `time-actions.ts`, `POST /api/time/:day/post`, `work timesheet post`): only on your click. Tempo v4 as the timesheet tool posts (issue id via `acli` — cached in state.db `meta` `time:issue-ids` —, start times from 09:00, no description), but Tempo's day is read first (`GET /4/worklogs/user/{account}`) and planned (`planDay`, pure): work's own earlier worklogs (`TimeDayRecord.posted.worklogs`) are kept when still wanted, removed when not — matched as Tempo has them now: one you changed by hand there (its time or issue) is yours from then on, never kept as unchanged nor deleted; an issue id Jira can't give now is the one work posted it under; worklogs you made by hand are never touched, and one that already covers a row (same issue, same time) means it isn't posted again — one on the same issue for another time stops that row (`differsByHand`: Tempo would have the issue twice; said in the outcome); only the rest is posted, starting where what stays ends (by its start time in Tempo now; never at 24:00 or later, which Tempo refuses). The token is the env variable `TEMPO_API_TOKEN` (or `time.tempo.tokenEnv`), your account `time.tempo.accountId` (or `JIRA_ACCOUNT_ID`); neither reaches the SPA (`tempoSetup`; the day carries only `posting: {ready, why}`). Posting pins the rows posted as yours (`editedIfUnset`: only on a day that still has none, so a save made while Tempo was called wins, and only when something of it is in Tempo now), so a later rebuild shows a new suggestion beside them instead of turning the day `changed`. A day then reads `posted` (in Tempo), or `changed` once its rows differ; a row that failed isn't recorded as posted, and an old worklog that couldn't be removed is recorded as still there — its ticket gets no new one meanwhile (Tempo would have both). Posts of one day run one after another (`postStoredDay`): two at once would both post the same rows. A posted day emptied since can be taken out of Tempo (the button says so). A Tempo day that can't be read changes nothing (502).

**Outlook and Teams** (`graph.ts`; `GET /api/time/graph`, `POST …/graph/connect|disconnect`; the tab's line "Outlook & Teams"): Microsoft Graph as you, by the device-code sign-in the timesheet tool uses (a code to enter at microsoft.com; the app registration `time.graph.clientId`/`tenantId`, else `GRAPH_CLIENT_ID`/`GRAPH_TENANT_ID`), read only (`Calendars.Read`, `Chat.Read`). The refresh token lives in `~/.work/graph-token.json` (owner-only), never in the SPA; new tokens are written before anything else can fail (`keep`: a refresh rotates the refresh token); a refresh only updates a sign-in still there (`updateTokens`, under the lock: Disconnect meanwhile wins), and one refused after another refresh rotated the token takes that one's tokens instead of saying "connect again". A refused refresh (expired, revoked) is recorded there and the line says "connect again" (`graphProblem`, `TimeGraphWire.problem`; also when `time.graph` names another app than the one signed in); a sign-in given up on (Disconnect, or Connect again) keeps nothing if its code is entered later. Chats and their messages are read page by page back to the day's start (`MAX_PAGES`); a meeting and a chat carry Outlook's / Teams' id (untitled chats share a name). Every call to Microsoft has a timeout (`TIMEOUT_MS`), and only the token endpoint's own refusal (`invalid_grant` and the like) means "connect again" — throttling or a server error is tried again next time. A day's meetings (every page — cut at `MAX_PAGES`, a failed read; times in the zone Graph gives them, `graphTime`, UTC when it ignored ours; not cancelled, declined, free or all day; each clipped to the day, time two share counted once) and the Teams chats you wrote in (your messages, shortened). Connecting rebuilds today.

**The AI step** (`classify.ts`, in `buildDay`): what the day couldn't place by itself — a session with no key in its branch or name, a commit naming none, a meeting, a chat — goes to the summarising agent (`runInternal`, no tools, small model) with the day's candidate tickets (the day's own keys, your assigned issues, the gap and time-off tickets), fenced as data; its answer is read strictly (`parsePlacement`: only items asked about, only candidate keys) and marked `guessed` (the tab's "AI"). It's asked again only when the unplaced items change (`unplacedHash` over what identifies them, not minutes or chat text, which move all day; item ids come from that too — `itemIds` —, never a place in a list sorted by minutes). A meeting placed on a ticket counts 1:1 (`TicketActivity.direct`); meetings left unplaced (or on the gap ticket) keep their room in the gap ticket's share (`suggestDay` `reserveMinutes`).

**What the timesheet tool had** (config `time`, `validateTime`): `hints` (`hints.ts`, pure: its TICKET_HINTS — a key and the words that mean it, matched in a session's name and branch, a commit's subject, a meeting's subject, a chat's name and your messages, when they name no key; `placeholder` for a ticket still to create in Jira: `TimeDayWire.placeholders`, said on its row, the hint's summary as its title; hint keys count as your projects and are the AI step's candidates too), `effort` (`suggestDay`: Claude minutes 1:1 in steps, no minimum beyond a step, a ticket only touched gets nothing), `vacation` (days off whatever was ticked: `TimeDayWire.vacation`, the box fixed), `catchUpDays` (the keeper's reach and the work-time reader's, `minutesFrom`; up to 92 — Claude Code keeps its transcripts about 30 days). Each build asks Jira about every one of the day's tickets but placeholders (`titles` → `titlesOf`: one search, and when one key Jira doesn't have fails it, each key alone, three at a time, the refused ones left out of the searches for an hour; its title and whether it's done → `TimeDayRecord.resolved`, "resolved" on its row; not asked: what the day had stands). Jira's evidence is your status changes and whatever else you updated (`jiraOn`: `updatedBy("<account>", "day 00:00", "next day 00:00")` — the status search too: a change in a day's last minute is on it —, a failure of it failing the whole lookup (half an answer would drop issues) — acli refuses `currentUser()` inside it; the account from `time.tempo.accountId`, `JIRA_ACCOUNT_ID`, else `myAccountId`). A meeting whose subject names a ticket, and a chat where a ticket was named that day (`TimeChatEvidence.mentions`: keys only from others' messages — their words are neither kept nor given to the AI step), are on it without the AI step (`unplacedItems` skips what has its own key). Your assigned issues (the AI step's candidates, and projects) are kept with the day (`TimeDayRecord.assigned`) and used again when they can't be read. Chats are read once for a range (`chatsSince`; a chat busier than `MAX_PAGES` reached back only so far, so the days it may have missed are refused — `ChatsRead.incompleteThrough` — and keep what they had), their messages paged by last change (Graph's order: an old message edited today is listed first): a catch-up is one read, not one per day. Not taken over: the tool's fixed project list (set `time.projects`) and its hard cutoff date.

**Ask about this day** (the tab's button, Ctrl+K): while the Time tab is on screen the SPA posts its day with the view (`AssistantView.day`); the stored context keeps only the date, and the prompt hook describes that day as it is at each prompt (`assistantContextDay`, `describeDayNow`: a Save, Post or rebuild since shows at once), in words (`describeTimeDay`: rows, the suggestion when edited, the evidence — fenced between `<<<` and `>>>` as data, never instructions, since others write some of it (titles, subjects, chat names) — each through `asData` (`fence.ts`: one line, no fence marker, so a subject can't close the fence early); the AI step's prompt fences its candidate titles the same way — and how to change it). `work timesheet show` is on `ASSISTANT_ALLOW`; `set`/`reset`/`off`/`post` are not (it asks), and posting is the user's to ask for.
